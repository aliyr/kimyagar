Use case: style-transfer and precise-object-edit

Primary request: Apply four coordinated revisions to Image 1.

1) STYLE — make the whole scene approximately 20–25% more stylized and production-friendly. Shift from semi-realistic concept painting toward unmistakable 2D stylized painterly game art informed by Persian miniature spatial logic and manuscript illustration. Slightly flatten the perspective, simplify planes, use broader confident painted shapes and controlled soft outlines, reduce micro-texture, make silhouettes more distinctive. Redesign bottles, mortar, cauldron, shelving and arches with gently exaggerated, memorable shapes. Keep material identity but simplify surface noise. It must look feasible to reproduce across hundreds of 2D mobile assets. Do not become childish, chibi, flat vector art, or cel-shaded.

2) UNIQUE IRANIAN WORLD SIGNATURE — make this specifically the visual world of “Kimiyagar,” not a generic Middle Eastern alchemy shop. Build a coherent proprietary design language from culturally grounded Iranian forms, used sparingly and functionally:
- bottles have a recurring stepped shoulder and softly lobed neck silhouette inspired by Iranian vessels;
- bottle labels are small pointed-arch/almond shapes with simple nonverbal pigment marks, no readable text;
- herb packets are folded like small manuscript folios, tied with restrained barberry-crimson cord and a small turquoise seal;
- the open recipe book has a lapis manuscript border with a tiny antique-gold eight-petal rosette;
- the table front and key tool rims repeat the same simplified eight-petal/pointed-arch family as subtle inlay and engraving;
- architecture uses integrated shallow niches and controlled geometric/vegetal rhythm, not pasted-on tile decoration.
These signatures should recur enough to feel authored, but never cover every surface.

3) GAMEPLAY COMPOSITION — remove roughly 30% of decorative objects and shelf clutter. Organize the scene into four instantly readable functional zones without modern UI overlays:
- a clear empty arched service bay / standing area at the rear-left where a customer can appear;
- one calm, grouped ingredient-pickup shelf with a few large, separated herb packets and bottles;
- the open recipe book on its own uncluttered angled stand at the lower-left;
- a strong left-to-right interaction chain on the main table: ingredient bowls and mortar -> clear preparation space -> large central copper cauldron.

Make each interactive prop readable at thumbnail/mobile size. Background decoration must be quieter, with larger grouped shapes and fewer tiny items. No characters.

4) COLOR ROLES — keep warm wood brown and parchment neutral as the base, but assign the identity colors more clearly and purposefully, without making the image more saturated:
- lapis blue defines the main structural arch, shelf backing accents, and book border;
- turquoise marks active alchemy, seals, and a few glass vessels;
- barberry crimson marks herb cords and one restrained textile accent;
- herbal green belongs to ingredient leaves and the living plant;
- antique gold marks interaction rims, tool edges, the book rosette, and tiny focal inlays.
Distribute these colors in a clear visual hierarchy, not evenly. Reduce the overall brown dominance while retaining warm atmosphere.

Composition/framing: mostly frontal with slight three-quarter depth; balanced landscape gameplay staging; visual hierarchy must read in this order: central copper cauldron, mortar/ingredient chain, open recipe book, ingredient shelf, empty customer bay, then decoration.

Lighting/mood: keep the same warm, soft, contemplative oil-lamp/window mood, but use cleaner value grouping so gameplay objects separate from the background. The only magical effect is a modest turquoise glow inside the cauldron and one extremely subtle gold response in the book motif.

Constraints/invariants: preserve the recognizable shop identity, human warmth, old handmade tactile materials, restrained noble magic, and absence of people. No text, no labels with letters, no logos, no watermark. Maintain a polished game-art concept sheet quality.

Avoid: semi-photorealistic rendering, photographic glass/copper highlights, dense realism, tiny high-frequency texture, equal-detail-everywhere, clutter, generic Western wizard shop, steampunk, Arabian Nights cliché, superficial “oriental” symbols, excessive tiles, excessive carpets, neon glow, strong bloom, hard cinematic contrast, modern UI overlays, arrows, icons, outlines around interactive zones, childish cartoon style.
